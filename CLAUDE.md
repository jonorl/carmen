# Where in the World? (family edition)

A remake of the 1990s DOS game "Where in the World is Carmen Sandiego?". The thieves are the player's family and friends instead of V.I.L.E. henchmen, and the country facts are updated. Written in Rust with macroquad, styled after the 1990s DOS version (black screen, double-line white frames, pixel font, a picture on the left, a text panel on the right, four icon buttons). A recorded printer sound plays while the printer prints. Ranks work as in the original: arrests promote the detective, and higher ranks get harder cases.

The game was first written in Python (`carmen.py`, removed when it moved to Rust; see git history). The Rust port keeps the same rules, timings and data files.

## Files

- `src/game.rs`: the rules, with no drawing code. Constants (`RANKS`, `PLACES`, timings) are at the top, with `rank_for` and `promotion_news`.
- `src/data.rs`: loads and validates the JSON files.
- `src/main.rs`: command-line options, the headquarters screen (`Hq`, `Step`) and the case screen with its text-panel modes (`Panel`).
- `src/computer.rs`: the crime computer drawing (monitor, stand, case); returns the screen area for the menu.
- `src/progress.rs`: each detective's arrests by name (`Progress`), saved at every arrest: a JSON file in the user's data folder on desktops, the browser's local storage on the web through `web/storage.js` (a miniquad plugin providing `carmen_storage_get` and `carmen_storage_set`), and on the server. Signing in starts a look-up (`look_up`); each arrest sends the device's tally (`add_arrest`); `poll`, every frame, takes the higher of the two, and after a look-up sends the device's if it's higher (arrests made offline). The server's address is `CARMEN_API` at build time (default the live one; empty for none).
- `src/net.rs`: requests that never block a frame (`Request::get`, `put`, polled each frame): a thread with `ureq` on desktops, `fetch` through `web/net.js` in browsers (a miniquad plugin: `carmen_http_start`, `_state`, `_take`, `_forget`), with an 8-second timeout.
- `shared/`: the crate the game and server share: `MAX_NAME` (8), name matching (`key`, `tidy`) and the JSON shapes (`Detective`, `Tally`).
- `server/`: the agency's records, an axum server over Postgres (sqlx; migrations in `server/migrations/`, applied on start). `GET /detectives/{name}` gives their arrests (0 if new), `PUT /detectives/{name}` with `{"arrests": n}` keeps the higher of that and what it had (`GREATEST`), so retries and offline devices can't lower a tally. No accounts: a name is a detective. Env: `CARMEN_DB_URL`, `PORT_CARMEN` (8085), `FRONTEND_URL_CARMEN` (CORS origins, comma-separated). Runs on the owner's VPS (the `my-vps` repo: `projects/docker-compose.yml`, Caddy at `carmen-api.jonathan-orlowski.dev`, the shared Postgres, nightly dumps to R2).
- `server/Dockerfile`: built from the repository root (`docker build -f server/Dockerfile .`), since the server needs `shared/`; `.dockerignore` keeps the game's assets out.
- `.github/workflows/`: `deploy-server.yml` (image to ghcr, then pulled on the VPS, like the owner's other projects) and `deploy-web.yml` (`build-web.sh`, then `wrangler pages deploy` to the Pages project `carmen`).
- `src/printer.rs`: the printer (at headquarters, and beside the crime computer for warrant results): queued text prints letter by letter onto tractor-feed paper, wrapped at 23 columns.
- `src/sound.rs`: sounds cut from recordings in `assets/sounds/`: `printer.ogg` loops while a printer prints (`Sounds::printing`, called every frame from `Printer::update`), `key.ogg` for each key typed, `tick.ogg` for each hour the clock moves, `walking.ogg` (five footsteps, `WALK_SECONDS`) on the way to a witness, `chase.ogg` under the chase, after the chase `footstep.ogg` (one step, 0.667 s, looped) while the walkers cross to jail, then with the suspect caught `captured.ogg` (music over the jail, 14.2 s) and `clank.ogg` (the prisoner grabbing the bars); when the suspect got away, `disappoint.ogg` (a sad tune before the officer's walk, cut where his first footstep broke it off). The capture recording's tail after the clank was the printer, matching `printer.ogg`, so the printer's own sound covers it. `henchman-stride.ogg`, `henchman-sneak.ogg` and `henchman-popup.ogg` (each V.I.L.E. henchman's tune), `danger-axe.ogg`, `danger-knife.ogg` and `danger-gun.ogg` (each final-city warning's sound and tune), `computing.ogg` while the crime computer works, `travelling.ogg` (5 s, looped by `Sounds::travelling` every frame) from take-off until landing, when the map gives way to the city. `Sounds::hush` stops the scenes when leaving a case. Every sound is Ogg Vorbis (`is_ogg_vorbis` checks), which keeps the web download small: convert new recordings with `ffmpeg -i in.wav -c:a libvorbis -q:a 4 out.ogg`.
- `assets/sounds/`: cut from recordings supplied by the owner with ffmpeg, mixed to mono 44.1 kHz and stored as Ogg Vorbis (quality 4), which keeps each file's exact length so the loops stay seamless: printer.ogg (0.7 s from a printer burst), key.ogg (one key press), tick.ogg (one clock tick), walking.ogg (the whole 1.86 s recording), and the rest as described under `src/sound.rs`.
- `src/ui.rs`: palette, pixel text (with fake bold and a fallback font), frames, buttons, the four icons and the mouse pointer.
- `src/scene.rs`: the city postcards' toolkit: `Painter` (draws with the time of day's lighting) and reusable parts (sky, clouds, mountains, hills, water, grass, buildings with windows, palms and other trees, people), plus a generic scene for countries without their own.
- `src/landmarks.rs`: one scene per country, matched by country name in `paint()`, each built around a landmark (pyramids, Eiffel Tower, Opera House, Angel Falls, Atomium, the Monkey Head...). A new country falls back to the generic scene until it gets a function here.
- `src/places.rs`: a 104x68 pixel-art picture for each place in `PLACES` (bank, airport, ...), matched by name; a new place needs a drawing here or it shows blank.
- `src/people.rs`: 66x96 portraits: one per witness in `PLACES`, and one per suspect for the dossiers. A suspect's photo in `assets/suspects/<photo_name>.png` wins; otherwise one is drawn (`suspect_look` reads hair colour and style, beards and glasses from the words in their traits; skin from the optional `skin` field, else from the name). Each is a `Look` (skin, hair, hat, glasses, beard, outfit) painted from one head-and-shoulders template; a new witness needs an entry in `look()` or gets a plain default.
- `src/sprites.rs`: the chase's running characters (the suspect, three police) the walk to jail's (the suspect with hands up and an officer with a pistol, or the officer slumped and alone) the three V.I.L.E. henchmen (one striding across, one sneaking past with a sack, and `PopUp`, who rises from the bottom edge, looks right and left, grins and sinks), and the final city's warnings (`Danger`: an axe spinning in to stick in the right-hand edge, a knife thrown into the left-hand edge, a hand cocking a pistol and firing a shot across). Each `Anim` is a strip of same-size frames from `assets/sprites/`, drawn at double size on the bottom edge of the text panel and clipped to its sides, stepping at the original's pace (ticks a second, pixels a tick, the frame for each tick of the cycle); `Cast` holds all four.
- `assets/sprites/`: the original game's frames, traced from the owner's recordings: each video frame sampled on the original 320x200 grid at the centre of every pixel, snapped to the original's VGA palette (the jail, the henchmen and the warnings have palettes of their own; with corrections for colour smeared by video compression on one-pixel yellow details), and each frame of the cycle taken as the per-pixel majority over its repeats. The police match a lossless screenshot exactly. Black is transparent.
- `src/jail.rs`: the jail picture after the chase (brick wall, sign, barred window): three 126x121 frames in `assets/sprites/jail.png` (the prisoner, the prisoner gripping the bars, the empty cell), traced from the recordings like the sprites with the jail's own palette, drawn at double size.
- `src/pixels.rs`: the small software canvas both use (rects, discs, triangles, dithering, a 3x5 sign alphabet), turned into hard-edged textures.
- `src/map.rs`: the departure map: Natural Earth land rasterised for a view fitted around the current city and its flights.
- `src/rng.rs`: a small seedable RNG (SplitMix64), so `--seed` replays a case without the `rand` crate.
- `src/touch.rs`: the name box for phones (see The window).
- `web/`: `index.html`, `storage.js` (saving progress, see `progress.rs`), `input.js` (the phones' name box, see `touch.rs`), `net.js` (web requests, see `net.rs`) and `mq_js_bundle.js` (macroquad's JS loader, copied from the macroquad 0.4.16 crate; recopy it from `~/.cargo/registry/src/*/macroquad-<version>/js/` when upgrading macroquad).
- `build-web.sh`: builds the browser version into `dist/` (git-ignored).
- `suspects.json`: suspects (the owner's family and friends), trait definitions and witness sentence templates.
- `countries.json`: 33 countries (one of them the fictional Monkey Island, in the Caribbean) with about 220 clues, grouped by type (flag, currency, language, landmark, geography, culture), plus city coordinates, an `about` line and stealable treasures.
- `assets/ne_110m_land.geojson`: Natural Earth 1:110m land outlines (public domain), drawn as the world map.
- `assets/fonts/`: Pixelify Sans (the pixel font), VT323 (the printer's monospaced font, crisp at 20 px) and Noto Sans Regular (only for ₹, which Pixelify lacks; see `MISSING` in `ui.rs`). All SIL OFL; licences alongside.
- `assets/suspects/`: dossier photos, 66x96 PNGs named like `jon-orlo.png` (`people::photo_name`). Made from real photos with `tools/portrait.sh PHOTO "Name" [CROP]` (ImageMagick: crop, shrink, 28 colours, no dithering). `build.rs` embeds whatever is there, so add a photo and rebuild. The source photos themselves are not kept in the repo.
- `build.rs`: generates `photos.rs` (the embedded photo list) into `OUT_DIR`.
- `tools/portrait.sh`: turns a photo into a dossier portrait.
- `.cargo/config.toml`: lets the wasm build leave quad-snd's browser audio functions undefined; `mq_js_bundle.js` supplies them at load time. Without it the web build fails to link.
- `README.md`: player and editor docs.

## Commands

- `cargo run`: play
- `cargo run -- --check`: validate the data files. Run this after any data edit.
- `cargo run -- --seed N --rank 1-6`: reproducible case, at that rank's difficulty whatever the saved rank
- `cargo test --workspace`: data validity, rules and a 300-seed-per-rank run of a careful player, plus the shared crate
- `cargo run -p carmen-server`: the server (needs `CARMEN_DB_URL`); `CARMEN_API=http://localhost:8085 ./build-web.sh` builds a game that talks to it
- `cargo build --release`: single binary in `target/release/`
- `./build-web.sh`: browser build in `dist/` (needs `rustup target add wasm32-unknown-unknown`); serve it with `python3 -m http.server -d dist 8000`

## Web build

`index.html` downloads `carmen.wasm` itself to show a loading bar, then hands it to macroquad's `load()`; `build-web.sh` writes the file's size into the page (`__WASM_BYTES__`), because a compressing web host's Content-Length is the compressed size. The download is about 4.3 MB, or 1.9 MB gzipped.

The JS console shows `register_plugin is not defined` from the bundle's networking plugin; it's a bug in that bundled plugin, harmless here. Browsers keep audio silent until the first key press or click.

macroquad compiles to `wasm32-unknown-unknown` and runs in a canvas through its own JS loader (no wasm-bindgen). Things that differ in the browser: `std::time::SystemTime` panics (use `miniquad::date::now()`), there is no filesystem (the embedded data is used) and no command-line options, and there is no Quit button (`CAN_QUIT`).

## Data loading

`--data DIR` if given; otherwise the current folder if it has `countries.json`; otherwise the copies compiled into the binary with `include_str!`. So a release binary can be handed out on its own, but it keeps whatever data it was built with: rebuild after editing the JSON.

## Architecture

- `Game` holds all rules: `investigate()`, `travel()`, `compute_warrant()`, `destinations()`, `give_up()`. It never draws or prints. Everything the player should read is pushed to `game.log` as `Line { tone, speaker, text }`; the front end styles each `Tone`.
- A case is generated in `Game::new`: the thief, a route of `legs + 1` countries, the treasure, a trait-reveal plan and the hideout place. Witness statements, places and decoy flights are generated lazily on first visit and cached, so the same seed plus the same actions replays identically. The GUI asks for places and destinations once on arrival in each city (`Case::refresh`).
- Trail city `i` has witnesses giving three distinct clues about `route[i+1]` (each place prefers certain clue types, see `PLACES`). Some witnesses add a trait sentence according to `trait_plan`. Wrong cities say "haven't seen anyone". In the final city, one of the three places is the hideout (the arrest); the other two give warnings plus a trait.
- `needed_traits()` greedily finds the traits that single out the thief. `plan_traits()` makes sure every trait is revealed along the trail, then repeats the needed ones.
- Flights: decoys exclude all route countries. Wrong cities always offer a flight back to the last trail city. Destinations are sorted by city name so the order gives nothing away.
- Time: the clock starts Monday 09:00. Interviews cost 3 hours each, flight time is distance / 800 km/h + 1 hour (rounded half-to-even, as Python's `round` did), and sleep runs 23:00 to 07:00. The deadline is computed per case (`compute_deadline`): a careful player's par time plus `spare_detours` average detours plus a `buffer`, set per rank in `RANKS`.
- Ranks (`RANKS`): Rookie, Sleuth, Private Eye, Investigator, Ace Detective and Super Sleuth, reached after 0, 1, 3, 6, 10 and 15 arrests, each setting the case's `legs`, `spare_detours` and `buffer`. Headquarters shows the detective's rank (`rank_for` of their saved arrests) and starts a case at it; `--rank` overrides the difficulty only. The arrest is counted and saved the moment the thief is caught (`Case::promotion`), and `promotion_news` is printed at the end of the case.
- Suspects have a `sex` (male or female): witnesses always say he or she (`Suspect::pronoun`), and the crime computer's first field is Sex, as in the original. Traits with `"computer": false` (Other) are witness clues and dossier lines only: they're left out of the warrant, `needed_traits` and the check that everyone can be told apart, but `plan_traits` still has witnesses mention them. A trait's `phrases` map a value to the words witnesses use (`Trait::phrase`). The validator warns when someone is the only man or the only woman, since the first witness gives them away.

## The window

- Two layouts (`Layout`, read through `lay()`): `WIDE` (640x360, as in the original: clock and picture on the left, text panel and buttons on the right) and `TALL` (352x702, for phones held upright: clock and picture above, text panel and buttons below, with the investigate window over the picture and the dossier stacked, photo above facts). Each frame picks `TALL` when the window is taller than it's wide. Every part keeps its size in both, so only positions change; draw through `lay()`, never fixed coordinates. Everything is drawn at the layout's size into its render target, then scaled to the window with nearest-neighbour filtering: whole-number scales from 2x up, an exact fit below that. The render target comes out upside down, hence `flip_y` when drawing it. `Ui::begin_frame` converts the mouse into layout coordinates; `Ui::to_window` goes the other way.
- Phones' on-screen keyboards only open for real page inputs, so on touch screens `web/input.js` puts an HTML text box over the headquarters name field while it's being typed (`touch::want`, applied by `touch::end_frame`); the game reads it with `touch::typed` and its Done key with `touch::entered`. Elsewhere those do nothing and the game reads the keyboard itself. macroquad turns taps into mouse clicks. The system cursor is hidden and a pixel arrow is drawn instead.
- Immediate mode: every frame redraws everything; buttons return true on the frame they're clicked, and `take_click` consumes the click so one click can't press two things.
- Layout constants (`CLOCK`, `PICTURE`, `TEXT`, the button row) are at the top of `main.rs`. The text panel shows one `Panel` at a time: briefing, story (what just happened, typed out at `TYPING_SPEED`), investigate, depart, flying, notes, crime computer, dossiers, help, give up. The picture shows the city's postcard, or the map while departing or flying.
- Investigating opens the original's pop-up window (`place_picker`): three place pictures with their names below, the hovered one highlighted, visited ones grey. While it's open, `ui.enabled` is false for the screen underneath, so only the window takes the mouse and keys; clicking outside or Escape closes it.
- A menu bar runs across the top (`MENUS`, `App::menu_bar`): Game (new case, how to play, quit), Options (sound) and Dossiers (every suspect). It takes the mouse before the screen does, and while a menu is open the screen underneath has `ui.enabled` false. A dossier (`App::dossier`, drawn by `dossier_window`) is a white sheet over whatever screen is showing, like the original's: name on a tab, photo at double size in a heavy frame, bold labels with VT323 answers, the bio as "Other". The screen underneath ignores input; the arrow keys leaf through files and a click or Escape closes it.
- The crime computer (`Panel::Crime { editing }`) draws the menu on the monitor's screen in yellow capitals, the hovered row in inverse video. Clicking a field lists its values (`editing: Some(field)`); the mouse wheel over a field steps through them. COMPUTE first sets `Case::computing`: for `COMPUTE_SECONDS` (2.1 s, the length of `computing.ogg`) the lights on the computer's stand flash random EGA colours and the menu ignores input; then `Action::Computed` runs the warrant and its verdict prints on the case's own printer in the picture frame, which starts each case with "READY.". With nothing entered, the verdict comes at once.
- Choosing a new place in the picker starts a walk (`Case::walking`): the picker stays open with the place highlighted while eight small footprints appear from the list up to its picture, in time with the footsteps sound; then `Action::Arrive` runs the interview (a click or key skips; places already visited skip the walk). The clock box shows `Case::shown_t`, which catches up with `game.t` an hour at a time, ticking for each, at a steady `HOUR_SECONDS` per hour, or half that for hours asleep (`game::asleep`) or in the air (`Case::flown`).
- When an interview corners the thief (`game.showdown`), `Panel::Chase` plays first, timed to `assets/sounds/chase.ogg` (17.6 s): the suspect sets off at `SUSPECT_AT` (1.6 s), "There goes the suspect!" shows 5.7-10.6 s, the police set off at `POLICE_AT` (14.5 s); the text sits low in the panel and its first line goes first, as in the original; then `Action::EndChase`. A click or key skips it.
- `Panel::March` and then `Panel::Jailed` follow, timed from the original's videos. Their sounds are cues (`CAUGHT_WALK`, `ESCAPED_WALK`, `CAUGHT_JAIL`) fired by time from `text_panel`, `cued` counting those played. With the right warrant: footsteps only while the suspect and an officer cross the bottom of the panel from right to left (from `MARCH_AT`, 0.2 s), the jail at `JAILED_AT` (9.8 s) with the music, the clank and his hands on the bars at `CLANK_AT` (14.2 s after the jail appears), then at `PRINT_AT` (14.75 s) the picture frame turns into the printer and starts on the ending. With the wrong warrant or none (`!game.won`): the sad tune over the empty panel from 1.0 s, the officer trudging across alone to his footsteps (from `TRUDGE_AT`, 7.1 s), then the empty cell at `EMPTY_JAIL_AT` (15.1 s) with the printer starting at once and no music. A click or key skips the walk to the jail, and the capture music to the clank; `Sounds::quiet` stops whatever was playing. The ending prints on a fresh `Printer` from `Panel::Jailed::ending`, skipping the first line (the spotting, which the chase has shown). `Printer::print_paragraphs` rests a second between paragraphs, as the original did, and pauses for the player whenever the next paragraph would push unread lines off the paper; a click or key finishes or carries on, and "New case" and "Quit" appear under the jail when it's done.
- Before the first interview in each city on the trail the player has flown to, except the first and the final city, `Game::investigate` sets `game.henchman` to one of the original's three V.I.L.E. henchmen at random, and whether he's the case's first. The front end then shows `Panel::Henchman` before the interview (`then`): his tune at `HENCHMAN_TUNE_AT` (0.9 s after the walk), him a moment later (`HENCHMAN_APPEARS`), and, the first time only, "A V.I.L.E. henchman! You must be on the right track." for `HENCHMAN_LINE_SECONDS` after he's gone, all timed from recordings of the original. A click or key skips to the line, then to the interview; the buttons are off meanwhile.
- In the final city, every wrong place (not the hideout) gets a warning instead: `Game::investigate` sets `game.danger` to one of three at random, and `Panel::Danger` plays it before the witness: the weapon at `DANGER_AT`, its sound and tune (`DANGER_CUES`), and the witness taking over as the tune ends (`DANGER_WITNESS_AT`), all timed from recordings of the original. A click or key goes straight to the witness.
- An interview shows `Panel::Interview`: the witness portrait bottom left with their job under it in VT323, and the statement typing out in a white speech bubble that grows upwards. Other lines from the same action (the time, a night's sleep) go small at the top. The arrest and running out of time stay as `Panel::Story`.
- Headquarters runs Name → Checking → Assignment → Briefing: the printer prints a welcome and echoes the name as it's typed (up to `MAX_NAME`), waits up to `CHECK_SECONDS` (4 s) for the server's answer before printing the rank, then prints the chosen case's briefing one paragraph at a time, pausing (`Printer::pause`) for a key between paragraphs because only about ten lines fit on the visible paper. Prompts go in the right panel and the white strip under it says what to press. A key or click skips the printing. Finishing a case and choosing "New case" comes back here, skipping the name.
- Sounds load in a coroutine so the first frame isn't held up (decoding is asynchronous in browsers). Key strikes are capped at one per 45 ms (`MIN_GAP`) so fast printing sounds like brisk typing. The on/off toggle is in the headquarters panel and the case notes.
- Screens return an `Action`; `App::apply` is the only place that changes game state. Flights animate first, then `game.travel()` runs on landing.
- Postcards are 274x282 and drawn pixel by pixel. Randomness is seeded from the country name, so a city always looks the same. The time of day sets the sky and lighting: draw through `Painter` methods for lit colours, and straight onto `p.cv` for light sources (lit windows, lamps, neon) that shouldn't darken at night. They're cached per (country, time of day).
- Pixelify Sans is crisp at 16 and 13 px (`BIG`, `SMALL`); other sizes blur. Bold is faked by drawing twice, one pixel apart.

## Calibration (simulated 400 cases per cell, done on the Python version)

A player who interviews all three witnesses in every trail city can always narrow the warrant to one person. With 2 interviews per city it works about 98-100% of the time; with 1, about 65-85%. Win rate for that full-interview player, by wrong turns taken:

| Difficulty (before ranks: Detective was Private Eye's settings) | 0 wrong | 1 wrong | 2 wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rookie | 100% | ~100% | ~80% |
| Detective | 100% | ~80% | ~27% |
| Super Sleuth | 100% | ~12% | ~1% |

The 0-wrong column is covered by `a_careful_player_on_the_right_trail_always_wins`. If you change timing constants, re-run a similar simulation by driving `Game` headless in a test.

## Content rules for clues

- Never name the country or the arrival city (`--check` warns on this).
- Avoid facts that go stale: leaders, populations, records.
- Use `{They}/{they}/{Them}/{them}/{Their}/{their}`; they become he or she forms.
- Watch ambiguity across countries. Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania all use shillings; dollars, pesos and euros are shared; Spanish, Swahili and Portuguese span several countries. Those currency and language clues are left out where they'd be ambiguous.
- Clue facts were written from model knowledge and not individually source-checked. Spot-check them before relying on them.
- British spelling throughout (colour, favourite, practised).
